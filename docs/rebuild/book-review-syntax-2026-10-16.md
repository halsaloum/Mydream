## Audit decisions reflected in the lessons

1. Begin with a visible contrast before formal terminology: change `ik werk` to `ik werkte`, compare one complete noun phrase with its replacement, and show two concrete scenes for an attachment ambiguity. This follows the books' use of evidence and graded hypothesis building.
2. Keep form, grammatical function and participant role separate. A passive can change the subject function while keeping the affected participant, and putting an object first does not turn it into a subject.
3. Preserve diagnostic limitations. Omission is not a definition of adjuncthood; a failed movement/passive test alone does not disprove a group/object; a tree is a model of independently motivated boundaries.
4. Do not transplant English constructions into Dutch. English modal noninflection, do-support, zero relatives, `one`-substitution and English binding domains are comparison evidence, not Dutch classroom rules. The guides explicitly keep Dutch patterns and defer model-specific terminology.
5. Use full group and clause boundaries. Matrix and root are not synonyms in deeper embedding; a finite relative inside the subject has its own anchor; a nonfinite clause need not contain a persoonsvorm.
6. Explain control versus raising through roles before a theoretical tree: `Noor probeert te slapen`, `Noor lijkt te slapen`, and `Het lijkt te regenen`. Avoid making PRO, case assignment or abstract movement compulsory for a clear Dutch revision.
7. Separate grammaticality from processing. Long dependencies can be grammatical but difficult; a fixed maximum number of words or verbs is not a language-wide grammatical rule.

## Concrete corrections and preservation

- Corrected a genuine controlled-comparison error in the persoonsvorm lesson: `heeft` becomes `hebben`, rather than `hadden`, when only subject number changes. The old wording accidentally changed number and tense simultaneously.
- Corrected the description of `Noor heeft de uitleg rustig gelezen`: the object and manner phrase stand between the two verb forms; the subject `Noor` precedes `heeft` and is not one of the intervening pieces.
- Clarified the comma distinction around relatives: a comma *before* the relative distinguishes the supplementary reading; a final comma can also be appropriate for a restrictive relative, particularly when finite verbs meet. Existing example strings and grading keys remain unchanged.

## Dutch-specific primary verification

The supplied books are comparative syntax books, not full Dutch reference grammars. The following primary sources were newly opened and read to check Dutch-specific patterns rather than silently treating English patterns as Dutch facts:

- [ANS21.1.1.1: sentence poles](https://e-ans.ivdnt.org/topics/pid/ans21010101lingtopic): finite first pole versus subordinator-first embedded pattern; the second pole can be empty; verbal material and functions differ.
- [ANS9.6.4: separable verbs](https://e-ans.ivdnt.org/topics/pid/topic-14861121445160917): the separable particle can remain associated over distance.
- [ANS18.5.1.2: grouping versus nongrouping](https://e-ans.ivdnt.org/topics/pid/ans18050102lingtopic): dependency and infinitive construction matter beyond counting lexical forms.
- [ANS18.5.2.1.ii: replacement infinitive](https://e-ans.ivdnt.org/topics/pid/ans1805020102lingtopic): IPP including `willen` and construction-dependent exceptions.
- [ANS18.5.7.3.ii: two-verb clusters](https://e-ans.ivdnt.org/topics/pid/ans1805070302lingtopic): both auxiliary-participle and participle-auxiliary order in the stated pair, without a free permutation claim.
- [ANS21.7.2.1: dependent clauses](https://e-ans.ivdnt.org/topics/pid/ans21070201lingtopic): outside placement and information organization.
- [ANS8.6.1.2: uses of er](https://e-ans.ivdnt.org/topics/pid/ans08060102lingtopic), [presentative er](https://e-ans.ivdnt.org/topics/pid/ans08060301lingtopic), [prepositional er](https://e-ans.ivdnt.org/topics/pid/ans080604lingtopic), [quantitative er](https://e-ans.ivdnt.org/topics/pid/ans0806050201lingtopic): four uses, reconstruction, split dependencies, nominal number agreement and impersonal passive.
- [Taaladvies.net: commas with restrictive/supplementary relatives](https://taaladvies.net/komma-bij-beperkende-en-uitbreidende-bijvoeglijke-bijzinnen/): beginning comma differs from the ending comma. This refinement is added in the prose without altering graded example strings.
